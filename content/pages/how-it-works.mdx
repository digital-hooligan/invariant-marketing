How decisions move through the system.

Most organizations capture signals but lose the structure needed to convert those signals into accountable decisions.
The Scientia operating model defines a structured lifecycle that connects signals, context, decisions, and execution.
Scientia.io implements this lifecycle.

## The Decision Lifecycle

### 1. Signals

Organizations constantly receive signals:

- operational metrics
- external information
- research
- internal reports
- incidents and observations

Inside the platform, Solum structures signal capture and evidence collection.
Signals are preserved rather than disappearing in chat threads or isolated dashboards.

### 2. Context

Signals become meaningful when placed in context.
Pondus maintains contextual continuity, preserving:

- historical decisions
- relevant evidence
- related discussions
- environmental conditions

This allows teams to understand not only what happened, but why it matters now.

### 3. Decisions

Decisions represent explicit organizational choices.
Within the system, RadixOS structures decision governance and accountability, connecting decisions to:

- the signals that triggered them
- the context surrounding them
- the people responsible for them

Decisions remain reviewable and traceable over time.

### 4. Execution

Once decisions are made, execution must remain visible.
Custos instruments execution workflows, ensuring that the actions connected to decisions can be monitored and understood.
Execution becomes part of the decision history rather than disappearing after implementation.

## Lifecycle Outcome

The lifecycle creates a continuous chain:

**Signal -> Context -> Decision -> Execution**

Instead of fragmented information and forgotten reasoning, organizations maintain a clear operational memory of how decisions emerged and what they produced.
